01 · Orientation

Overview

Neuroscience connects processes operating at many scales, from hormones and genes to neural circuits and behaviour. This section collects concepts that cross traditional topic boundaries and help explain how the nervous system coordinates complex responses.

The current material introduces the biological stress response, the cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical circuit, and two cortical regions historically associated with language. These foundations can later support deeper sections on research methods and interpretation.

02 · Reference

Glossary

Hormones, circuits, and cortical regions used in the concepts below.

HPA axis
The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal system that coordinates an endocrine response to stress.
Corticotropin-releasing hormone
A hypothalamic hormone that signals the anterior pituitary during activation of the HPA axis.
Adrenocorticotropic hormone
A pituitary hormone that travels through the circulation and stimulates the adrenal cortex.
Cortisol
A glucocorticoid hormone released by the adrenal cortex as part of the stress response.
CSTC circuit
A loop linking cortex, striatum, thalamus, and cortex through direct and indirect basal-ganglia pathways.
Broca's area
A frontal cortical region historically associated with speech production and expressive language.
Wernicke's area
A temporal cortical region historically associated with language comprehension.

03 · Explore

Associated Concepts

System-level concepts that connect physiology, neural circuits, cognition, and behaviour.

Stress and the HPA Axis

The hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis translates a perceived stressor into a coordinated hormonal response:

  1. The hypothalamus releases corticotropin-releasing hormone (CRH) into the portal circulation serving the anterior pituitary.
  2. CRH stimulates the anterior pituitary to release adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) into the general circulation.
  3. ACTH stimulates the adrenal cortex to release cortisol.

Cortisol helps mobilize energy and adjust body systems to meet a challenge. The duration and regulation of this response are important because prolonged activation can affect cognition, mood, immune activity, sleep, and metabolism.

The CSTC Loop

The cortico-striato-thalamo-cortical loop connects regions of the cerebral cortex with the striatum, pallidal structures, and thalamus before returning information to cortex. The circuit helps regulate action selection, habits, motivation, and cognitive control.

In simplified terms, the direct pathway facilitates selected cortical activity by reducing inhibitory output to the thalamus, while the indirect pathway counterbalances that activity and helps suppress competing responses. Changes in CSTC signalling are studied in relation to conditions including obsessive-compulsive disorder.

Language-Associated Regions

Broca's area is classically associated with speech production, while Wernicke's area is classically associated with language comprehension. Modern neuroscience treats language as a distributed network, so these regions are important nodes rather than isolated language centres.
