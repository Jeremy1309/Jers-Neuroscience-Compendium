01 · Orientation

Overview

The brain is the control centre of the nervous system and is the most complex organ in the human body. It continuously receives information from both the external environment and the body itself, processes this information, and coordinates appropriate responses.

Although the brain weighs only approximately 2.6–3.1 lb (about 2% of total body weight), it contains an estimated 86 billion neurons connected by hundreds of trillions of synapses. Together, these cells form an intricate network capable of processing enormous amounts of information every second.

Rather than functioning as a single uniform structure, the brain is organized into numerous specialized regions and interconnected networks. Different areas are responsible for specific functions, such as processing sensory information, controlling movement, regulating emotions, forming memories, and making decisions. Despite individual brain regions having specialized roles, they rarely work in isolation; instead, complex behaviours and cognitive processes emerge through communication between many different areas of the brain.

Advances in neuroscience over the past century have dramatically improved our understanding of the brain. However, many aspects of this topic remain poorly understood, making it one of the most active and exciting areas of scientific research.

03 · Explore

Subtopics

Explore the brain from broad organization to individual cortical and subcortical regions.

The Four Cortical Lobes

  • Frontal lobe — executive functions, planning, and motor control.
  • Parietal lobe — somatosensory processing and spatial reasoning.
  • Temporal lobe — auditory processing, memory, and language.
  • Occipital lobe — vision.

Important Brain Regions

  • Hippocampus — memory formation.
  • Amygdala — emotion and fear processing.
  • Prefrontal cortex — higher cognition and planning.
  • Basal ganglia — motor control and habit learning.
  • Thalamus — sensory relay.
  • Hypothalamus — homeostasis and endocrine control.
  • Brainstem — vital functions and arousal.
  • Cerebellum — coordination and motor learning.
